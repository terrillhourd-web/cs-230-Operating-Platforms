Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?
The Gaming Room is a client that approached us to build Draw It or Lose It, which is a web-based multiplayer team guessing game. The software needed to support multiple teams with multiple players, enforce unique names both for teams and games, and have centralized control of the game's timing, scoring and overall state. We also needed to guarantee that only one active instance of the game would be in memory at a time, and that each game, team, and player would have unique, predictable identifiers for security and scalability.
What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?
I did a particularly good job designing a clear, organized, and technically sound design to fit the needs of the client. The Domain model section is where I really excelled by applying OOD concepts (inheritance, composition, and encapsulation) and discussing the relationships between the different classes and my use of Singleton as a centralized control over the game. The two final sections were also robust and provided realistic suggestions that operating platforms will have effects on performance and scalability.
What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
Writing the design doc made me slow down and plan out how the code would work and what each part would do and how the different parts would communicate and what constraints would be enforced. It forced me to make decisions before writing the code. Laying out all the requirements, constraints, and domain model in advance gave me something to aim for throughout the process. This left less room for confusion and helped me keep the client's goals in mind as I worked.
If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
Were I to make one change it would be to the System Architecture View. Not required for this project, a quick diagram or description of the logical architecture, how the clients, servers and databases talk to one another would be a nice addition that would help visualize the design. This would help bridge the gap between the conceptual design and the real deployment.
How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
I took into account the user's needs by designing the system around their primary objectives: a multiplayer, team-oriented game that was reliable, secure, and easy to manage. I incorporated these requirements by implementing unique identification, a Singleton design pattern for centralized control, and a clear separation of concerns that mirror the relationships between players, teams, and games. Taking the user's needs into account is crucial as software is developed to solve real-world problems, and neglecting them can result in a system that may work technically but fails to provide value or a good user experience.
How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?
I implemented the design of the software with an object-oriented and modular approach. I started by identifying the entities in the system and their relationships, then utilized design patterns such as Singleton for centralized management and composition for team-player hierarchies. In future design processes, I would continue to employ UML diagrams and structured design documentation to visualize relationships and ensure scalability. Additionally, I’d integrate more user feedback earlier in the process to validate that the design meets user expectations before development begins.
